# GreenPulse: A Community Climate Action and Environmental Monitoring Application

# Week 1 — Research and Validation

**SDG Alignment:** SDG 13 — Climate Action

## 1. Research Purpose

The research aims to understand how residents access climate-related information, recognize environmental changes, respond to climate-related risks, and participate in activities that promote environmental awareness and climate resilience.

It also seeks to identify the challenges people experience when finding reliable environmental information and discovering opportunities to take part in local climate action initiatives.

## 2. Research Method

The team will use surveys and, where possible, short interviews with potential GreenPulse users, including students, community volunteers, and residents.

The research will explore their awareness of climate change, preferred sources of environmental information, experiences with local environmental concerns, and interest in using a mobile application to support climate action.

Responses will be organized into common themes to help identify user needs, expectations, and opportunities for improvement. Any findings reported in the final documentation will be based on the actual feedback collected.

## 3. Key Findings

The actual survey and interview responses should be analyzed and summarized here after data collection.

- **Climate Awareness:** Summarize respondents' understanding of climate change and its effects on their communities.
- **Environmental Information:** Identify where respondents currently obtain weather updates, environmental information, and climate-related announcements.
- **Environmental Concerns:** Record the climate-related and environmental challenges respondents identify in their communities, such as flooding, extreme heat, or improper waste disposal.
- **Community Participation:** Summarize respondents' interest in activities such as tree planting, environmental education, waste reduction, and climate awareness campaigns.
- **Application Expectations:** Identify the features respondents consider useful, such as environmental information dashboards, climate education resources, community activity announcements, and environmental concern reporting.
- **Suggested Improvements:** Document recurring recommendations about accessibility, ease of use, information reliability, and features that would encourage continued engagement.

## 4. Impact on Project Design

The research results will guide the design and prioritization of GreenPulse features according to the needs of its intended users.

Potential design decisions include:

- Creating a clear dashboard for accessing climate-related information.
- Providing educational resources that explain climate change in understandable language.
- Organizing community climate action activities and announcements.
- Designing a straightforward process for reporting environmental concerns, if included in the project scope.
- Presenting environmental information clearly and identifying its source and update time where applicable.

The team will use the findings to refine the user personas, user flow, and low-fidelity wireframes. Features will be prioritized based on user feedback, project requirements, feasibility, and their relevance to SDG 13 — Climate Action.

## 5. Conclusion

Week 1 research and validation will establish an initial understanding of the intended users' climate awareness, environmental information needs, and interest in community-based climate action.

The collected evidence will help the GreenPulse team make informed design decisions and develop a prototype that responds to users' needs while supporting climate awareness and resilience.

The research conclusions and final feature priorities will be updated after the team reviews the actual survey and interview results.

# Week 2 — Component Design System & Clickable Interactive Figma Prototype

**SDG Alignment:** SDG 13 — Climate Action

## 6. Week 2 Objectives

Week 2 focuses on translating the initial GreenPulse concepts and Week 1 wireframes into a more consistent and interactive Figma prototype.

The team will establish a reusable component design system, improve the visual consistency of the application, and connect the main screens through clickable interactions.

The design process will prioritize accessibility, ease of navigation, consistent styling, and environmental features that support the objectives of GreenPulse.

## 7. Design Implications and Priorities

The initial research objectives and preliminary user personas will guide the design of the GreenPulse interface. Since the actual research findings must still be documented after data collection, these priorities will be refined as feedback becomes available.

### 7.1 Environmental Monitoring

The interface should make available environmental information easy to understand and navigate.

Planned design elements include:

- Environmental monitoring cards for available indicators such as temperature, rainfall, or air quality.
- Clear labels, measurement units, and descriptions where applicable.
- Source and update-time information for environmental readings, when available.
- Simple layouts that allow users to review environmental information without unnecessary complexity.

Actual readings will depend on the available data sources and implemented functionality.

### 7.2 Environmental Concern Reporting

The reporting interface should guide residents through the process of describing and submitting environmental concerns.

Planned design elements include:

- Concern category selection.
- Description and location input fields.
- Photo attachment options, if supported.
- Clear submission and cancellation buttons.
- Review and confirmation screens.
- Report status indicators, if report tracking is implemented.

The interface should help users understand what information is required before submitting a report.

### 7.3 Climate Awareness and Educational Resources

The application should present climate-related educational content in a readable and accessible format.

Planned design elements include:

- Climate awareness cards.
- Short educational descriptions and sustainability tips.
- Organized educational topics.
- Readable typography and clear visual hierarchy.
- Navigation controls for opening and returning from educational content.

The content should help users understand climate change and identify practical actions that can support environmental protection.

### 7.4 Climate Action Activities

The activity interface should help users discover community programs and environmental initiatives.

Planned design elements include:

- Activity cards displaying available names, schedules, and locations.
- Activity detail screens.
- Participation instructions.
- Registration or interest buttons, if supported.
- Consistent icons and layouts across activity-related screens.

These components aim to make climate action opportunities easier to discover and understand.

## 8. Component Design System

The team will establish a consistent visual design system to support the development of reusable UI components across the GreenPulse application.

### 8.1 Color Palette

The existing green visual identity of GreenPulse will be maintained to reinforce its connection with environmental awareness and climate action.

The design system will define consistent colors for:

- Primary actions and buttons.
- Secondary actions.
- Screen backgrounds and content cards.
- Headings and body text.
- Report status indicators and feedback messages.

Color combinations should provide sufficient contrast and support readability.

### 8.2 Typography

A consistent typography system will be used across the application.

It will define:

- Heading styles for screen titles and major sections.
- Body text styles for descriptions and educational content.
- Supporting text styles for dates, locations, labels, and additional information.
- Consistent text sizes, weights, and line spacing.

Typography should remain readable across the different application screens.

### 8.3 Reusable UI Components

The proposed reusable components include:

- **Navigation Buttons:** Allow users to move between the Home Dashboard and major application features.
- **Environmental Monitoring Cards:** Display available environmental indicators and related information.
- **Climate Awareness Cards:** Present educational content and practical sustainability tips.
- **Climate Action Activity Cards:** Display activity descriptions, schedules, and locations.
- **Report Status Badges:** Indicate the current status of submitted environmental concerns, if supported.
- **Form Components:** Provide standardized input fields, category selectors, and action buttons.
- **Feedback Messages:** Communicate successful submissions, missing information, or errors where applicable.

Reusable components will help maintain visual consistency and simplify future design updates.

### 8.4 Layout and Spacing

The application will use consistent spacing, alignment, padding, and corner radii across screens.

The layout should provide a clear visual hierarchy, make important actions easy to identify, and avoid overcrowding information on smaller mobile screens.

### 8.5 Component Consistency

Components with similar functions should maintain consistent appearances and behavior throughout the application.

For example, primary buttons should follow the same visual style, while report status badges should use consistent labels and colors wherever they appear.

These standards will help users recognize familiar interface elements and navigate GreenPulse more easily.

## 9. Clickable Interactive Figma Prototype

The team will develop a clickable Figma prototype that demonstrates the intended navigation and interactions between the main GreenPulse screens.

### 9.1 Home Dashboard Navigation

The Home Dashboard will serve as the main entry point to the application's core features.

Users should be able to navigate to:

- Environmental Monitoring.
- Environmental Concern Reporting.
- Climate Action Activities.
- Climate Awareness and Educational Resources.

Each navigation element should lead to its corresponding screen when that interaction is included in the prototype.

### 9.2 Environmental Monitoring Interaction

The prototype should demonstrate how users select an environmental indicator and view its available details.

The flow should include navigation from the dashboard to the monitoring screen, selection of an indicator, and access to its information screen where applicable.

### 9.3 Environmental Concern Reporting Interaction

The prototype should demonstrate the intended reporting process, including:

1. Opening the reporting screen.
2. Selecting an environmental concern category.
3. Entering the concern description and location.
4. Reviewing the report information.
5. Proceeding to a confirmation screen in the prototype.

Report tracking and subsequent status changes should only be represented as functional features if they are included in the actual prototype.

### 9.4 Climate Action Activities Interaction

The prototype should allow users to open the activities section, browse available activity cards, and view activity details.

Registration or participation interactions may be included if supported by the design scope.

### 9.5 Climate Awareness Interaction

Users should be able to open educational cards, view climate-related content, and return to the previous screen or Home Dashboard using clear navigation controls.

### 9.6 Navigation and Return Controls

The prototype should provide consistent navigation patterns across the main screens.

Users should be able to return to the Home Dashboard or previous screen without confusion. Buttons and interactive elements should have clear labels and appropriate destinations.

## 10. Validation and Refinement

The team will review the GreenPulse design system and clickable prototype to identify opportunities for improvement.

The review will consider:

- Consistency of colors, typography, icons, buttons, and cards.
- Clarity of navigation and screen organization.
- Readability of environmental information.
- Ease of understanding the reporting process.
- Accessibility and usability of the interface.
- Correct connections between screens in the Figma prototype.

Where possible, the team will gather feedback from potential users to determine whether the design is understandable and whether the proposed features address their needs.

Feedback and identified design issues will guide further revisions. Any claims about user validation will be based on actual testing or feedback collected.

## 11. Expected Week 2 Deliverables

The expected Week 2 deliverables are:

- A consistent GreenPulse component design system.
- Defined color, typography, spacing, and layout standards.
- Reusable UI components for the application's main features.
- Updated screen designs based on the Week 1 low-fidelity wireframes.
- A clickable interactive Figma prototype connecting the main screens.
- Documented design refinements based on review and available user feedback.

The team should verify that these deliverables are actually completed before reporting them as finished.

## 12. Conclusion

Week 1 establishes the research objectives, preliminary user needs, and design direction for GreenPulse. Week 2 builds on that foundation by translating the initial concepts and wireframes into a consistent component design system and clickable interactive Figma prototype.

Together, these stages aim to create a user-centered application design that supports environmental awareness, access to environmental information, reporting of local environmental concerns, and community participation in climate action.

The GreenPulse design will continue to evolve as the team collects actual user feedback, evaluates the prototype, and prepares for the Week 3 mobile MVP scaffold and core feature development.

The project remains aligned with UN Sustainable Development Goal 13 — Climate Action.
